Vanilla Bean Syrup

Component Mass Calibration

Ingredient Mass Analytical Protocol Note
Water 533 grams Aqueous solvent base; ratio strictly balanced to preserve viscosity limits.
Allulose Classic 346 grams 1:1 sucrose sweetener equivalent; provides targeted body without crystallization.
Grade B Vanilla Beans 11 grams Approximately 3 to 4 whole beans. Must be split lengthwise and inner caviar scraped.
Vanilla Extract 11 grams Added post-thermal phase to retain volatile top-note aromatics.
Fine Sea Salt 2.7 grams Sodium chloride matrix addition to sharpen vanillin perception profiles.

Standard Operating Procedure

1. Caviar Preparation: Place a clean cutting board on the bench. Split the 11 grams of vanilla beans longitudinally with a sharp knife. Scrape the blade down the interior channel of each pod half to isolate the seed caviar.
2. Thermal Extraction: Combine the 533 grams of water, 346 grams of allulose classic, the isolated vanilla caviar, and the spent pod hulls in a saucepan. Bring the mixture to a low, steady simmer. Secure a tight-fitting **Lid On** the vessel immediately to prevent mass loss via evaporation. Maintain this extraction state for exactly 15 minutes.
3. Native Equilibration: Terminate the burner heat completely. Leave the lid secured to capture condensing aromatic fractions. Allow the pan to descend undisturbed until it reaches ambient room temperature, enabling maximum structural infusion of the vanillin compounds.
4. Final Fortification: Remove the lid. Introduce the 11 grams of vanilla extract and 2.7 grams of fine sea salt into the cooled syrup matrix. Whisk vigorously for 30 seconds until the salt crystals are entirely dissolved and the extracted compounds are uniform.
5. Packing Protocol: Decant the syrup into a clean, empty 750 ml Torani bottle using a small funnel. You may filter out the large pod hulls, but allow the fine black caviar specks to pass into the final bottle. The volume will align to leave roughly one inch of protective safety headspace, allowing clean insertion of the pump mechanism.
